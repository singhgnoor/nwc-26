#### Session 1: Inaugural Ceremony
*Day 1, Sunday, 6 December 2026 | 09.30–10.15 hrs*

**Brief:** The inaugural ceremony will formally open समरस्यम्' 2026, the 3rd National Well-being Conclave, hosted by the Indian Institute of Technology, Ropar on the theme "Mental Wellness, Social Harmony and Collective Development". It will set out the purpose, scope and intended deliverables of the two-day programme and affirm the shared commitment of the Ministry of Education and higher education institutions to advancing student wellbeing as an integral part of the academic mission.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will gain a clear understanding of the mandate, objectives and intended deliverables of the two-day programme.
- A shared sense of purpose will be established among delegates drawn from diverse institutional settings.
- Institutional and policy-level commitment to student wellbeing will be affirmed at the outset, setting the tone for the deliberations that follow.

#### Session 2: Wellness Exhibition Tour
*Exploring institutional best practices, feedback on exhibits and submission of key learnings · Day 1 | 10.15–11.20 hrs*

**Brief:** The Wellness Exhibition will bring together exhibits from participating institutions presenting their wellbeing initiatives, support structures and campus practices, including posters on institution-specific surveys of student wellbeing and the associated anonymised statistics. Delegates will tour the exhibits, interact with institutional representatives, offer feedback on the exhibits and record the key learnings they wish to take back to their own campuses. The learnings so submitted will form part of the documented record of the Conclave.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will be exposed to a wide range of wellbeing practices drawn from institutions of differing size, type and resource position.
- Exhibiting institutions will receive structured peer feedback on their initiatives.
- A compilation of key learnings and replicable practices will be drawn from delegates' submissions as an input to the valedictory session and the Conclave report.

#### Session 3: Presentation on IIT Ropar's Wellness Ecosystem
*The Well-being Model of IIT Ropar: Where Excellence Meets Emotional Wellness · Day 1 | 11.20–11.30 hrs*

**Brief:** This presentation will use IIT Ropar's institution-wide approach to student wellbeing as a practical model of an integrated campus ecosystem. It will outline the governance and organisational arrangements, counselling and support services, referral pathways, the roles of faculty and residential staff, and student-facing initiatives. It will also distinguish the model's transferable design principles from the context-specific choices shaped by IIT Ropar's implementation experience, constraints and subsequent adjustments.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will obtain a concrete, operational illustration of how the several elements of a wellbeing ecosystem are structured and sustained within IIT Ropar.
- Transferable design elements will be identified for adaptation to participants' own institutional contexts and resource positions.
- A common practical reference point will be established for the stakeholder breakaway deliberations on Day 1 and the presentations of institutional survey findings on Day 2.

#### Session 4: Presentation on Initiatives of the Department of Higher Education
*Day 1 | 11.30–11.45 hrs*

**Brief:** This session will provide an overview of the evolving policy and institutional landscape concerning student mental wellbeing in higher education. It will highlight the key initiatives undertaken by the Ministry of Education to strengthen wellbeing and support systems in HEIs. It will also present the National Well-being Conclaves as a platform for sharing institutional practices, fostering dialogue and translating policy into sustainable campus-level action.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will be able to situate campus-level wellbeing work within the current national policy framework and the initiatives of the Ministry of Education.
- Participants will identify the institutional obligations and enabling provisions arising from the Framework Guidelines and subsequent directions.
- Institutions will be better placed to translate policy intent into sustainable campus practice, informed by the documented learnings of the previous National Well-being Conclaves.

#### Session 5: Panel Discussion I
*Transforming social connectedness and belongingness in the emerging well-being climate of 21st-century HEIs · Day 1 | 11.45–12.30 hrs*

**Brief:** Social belonging is a critical determinant of student wellbeing and a key element of a supportive campus environment. Changing patterns of peer relationships, growing social isolation among young people and the increasing diversity of the student body make deliberate effort towards inclusion more necessary than before. This panel will examine the barriers to building connected and inclusive campuses, including stigma, limited awareness and hesitation in seeking support, and will consider the role of faculty mentorship, peer groups and student engagement in strengthening social harmony and a sense of belonging. Panellists will discuss practical approaches to building peer connection, improving awareness of available support spaces and fostering a culture in which seeking help is normalised.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will be able to identify the principal barriers to social belonging on their own campuses, including social isolation, stigma, limited awareness and reluctance to seek support.
- Participants will acquire practical approaches to strengthening peer connection, including peer mentoring and buddy programmes and inclusive student engagement initiatives.
- The essential elements of a campus belongingness framework will be identified, including the periodic assessment of campus connectedness.
- Institutions will be equipped to improve the visibility, accessibility and perceived safety of existing support spaces.

#### Session 6: Symposium
*Decoding healthy living: the science of sleep, nutrition and lifestyle · Day 1 | 12.30–13.15 hrs*

**Brief:** Student wellbeing extends beyond mental health to physical health and the daily habits that sustain both. This symposium will bring together experts in mental health, neurology, sleep medicine and nutrition to examine the scientific evidence on how sleep, diet and lifestyle shape mood, cognition, academic performance and long-term health among young adults. It will address the irregular sleep patterns, poor dietary practices and sedentary routines that are common in residential campus life, and will identify practical, low-cost measures that HEIs can adopt to create healthier campus environments.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will understand the evidence linking sleep, nutrition and physical activity with mental wellbeing and academic performance.
- Participants will understand the principal lifestyle-related and non-communicable health risks affecting young adults in residential campus settings.
- Participants will identify practical, low-cost campus measures across sleep hygiene, campus nutrition, physical activity and metabolic health.
- Institutions will be enabled to integrate physical health promotion within wellbeing provision rather than administering it as a separate function.

#### Session 7: Ice-breaking Session
*Day 1 | 14.00–14.15 hrs*

**Brief:** This short, interactive session will re-energise delegates after the lunch break and encourage informal interaction among participants drawn from different institutions and stakeholder groups. It will be followed by a briefing on the objectives, composition, format and expected deliverables of the four parallel breakaway sessions scheduled later in the afternoon.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Rapport and openness will be built among delegates, creating conditions conducive to candid deliberation.
- Participants will be clear about their breakaway group allocation, the questions to be deliberated and the format in which outcomes are to be presented.

#### Session 8: Panel Discussion II
*The campus mental health ecosystem: challenges, opportunities and institutional responsibilities · Day 1 | 14.15–15.00 hrs*

**Brief:** Students in higher education today face a widening range of mental health concerns, from academic pressure, anxiety and difficulties of adjustment to conditions requiring clinical care. This panel will examine these emerging challenges and the corresponding roles and responsibilities of institutional leadership, faculty, counsellors, wardens and student bodies. It will consider how counselling and referral systems may be strengthened, how early identification and preventive interventions can be built into the routine functioning of the campus, and how faculty may be sensitised to recognise distress and support students appropriately. The panel brings together perspectives from student welfare administration and faculty development across a central university, an IIT and a university teacher-training centre.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will develop a shared understanding of the emerging mental health challenges among students and of how responsibility for responding to them is distributed within an institution.
- The essential elements of an institutional mental health policy will be identified.
- The components of a standard operating procedure for early identification and referral, and of strengthened counselling facilities, will be outlined.
- Institutions will be encouraged to institute faculty and staff sensitisation programmes and periodic institutional wellbeing assessment.

#### Session 9: Panel Discussion III
*The impact of social media, youth psychology and digital decluttering · Day 1 | 15.00–15.45 hrs*

**Brief:** Digital technology is now woven into almost every aspect of student life, from learning to friendship and leisure. While it offers access and connection, excessive and unreflective use is associated with screen dependence, fear of missing out, social comparison, disturbed sleep and reduced academic focus. This panel will examine the impact of social media on youth psychology and the effects of digital overload on sleep and academic performance, and will discuss responsible technology use, digital decluttering and the promotion of offline engagement and healthy digital habits. It brings together clinical, academic and technology-industry perspectives.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will understand the psychological mechanisms through which social media and digital overload affect students' wellbeing, sleep and academic performance.
- The elements of a digital wellbeing framework for HEIs will be identified, including digital literacy and awareness programmes.
- Participants will acquire practical approaches to screen-time management and to the promotion of technology-free activities on campus.
- Institutions will be encouraged to adopt and model responsible digital engagement practices.

#### Session 10: Four Parallel Breakaway Sessions and Presentation of Stakeholder Deliberations
*Deliberation on strengthening campus well-being across HEIs · Day 1 | 16.00–16.45 hrs (Breakaway Sessions); 16.45–17.30 hrs (Presentation of Stakeholder Deliberations)*

**Brief:** Delegates will deliberate in four parallel stakeholder groups (Student Affairs, Faculty, Counsellors and Students) on strengthening campus wellbeing across HEIs. Drawing on the exhibition, presentations and panel discussions earlier in the day, each group will examine, from its own vantage point, the gaps in present arrangements, the practices that have worked, the support it requires from institutions and the Ministry, and the actions it can itself take.

Each group will then present its key outcomes and recommendations in plenary through a short presentation of about ten minutes, followed by discussion.

**Expected outcomes:**
- A stakeholder-specific assessment of gaps and priorities in campus wellbeing provision will be obtained.
- Each stakeholder group will formulate a set of concrete, actionable recommendations.
- Points of convergence and divergence between the perspectives of students and those of service providers will be identified.
- The consolidated recommendations will serve as an input to the valedictory roadmap and the Conclave report.

#### Session 11: Focused Workshop I
*Early identification and management of specific learning disabilities, substance-use prevention and related well-being concerns · Day 2, Monday, 7 December 2026 | 09.30–10.00 hrs*

**Brief:** This expert session will focus on identifying the early signs of specific learning disabilities and other concerns that may affect students' academic and psychosocial wellbeing. It will also address the prevention of substance use and related areas requiring early attention within HEIs. The session will equip participants with greater awareness of the mechanisms for identification, response and referral in supporting students.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will be able to identify flag signs of specific learning disabilities and of associated academic and psychosocial difficulty.
- Participants will understand preventive approaches to substance use and the referral pathways available within and outside the institution.
- Institutions will strengthen their identification, academic accommodation and referral mechanisms for students requiring specific support.

#### Session 12: Focused Workshop II
*Predictive well-being: digital tools and biomarkers for early identification of mental health concerns · Day 2 | 10.00–10.30 hrs*

**Brief:** Advances in behavioural science, digital technology and biomedical research are opening new possibilities for recognising the risk of mental health concerns before they become acute. This workshop will introduce participants to emerging digital tools, behavioural data analytics and biological markers being explored for early identification, drawing on perspectives from industry research and clinical practice. It will also examine the limits of such approaches and the safeguards their use in educational settings requires, including informed consent, data privacy, the avoidance of labelling, and the principle that every signal should lead to human contact and professional support rather than to automated judgement.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will gain awareness of the current state of evidence on digital tools and biomarkers for the early identification of mental health concerns.
- Participants will be able to apply basic criteria of validity, privacy, consent and ethics in evaluating such tools prior to adoption.
- It will be understood that predictive tools supplement, and do not replace, counselling services and professional judgement.
- Institutions will be better placed to consider responsibly designed, pilot-based use of such tools linked to established referral pathways.

#### Session 13: Student Presentations from the Life Skills Workshop
*Day 2 | 10.45–12.45 hrs*

**Brief:** Student participants will engage in a Life Skills Workshop, through interactions scheduled on 5 and 6 December 2026, exploring how life skills and peer mentoring can strengthen their ability to navigate academic, social and emotional challenges. In this session, student teams will present what they have learnt and developed during the workshop, demonstrating practical tools and approaches for building supportive peer relationships and social-emotional partnership. The session places the student voice at the centre of the Conclave and illustrates how students can support one another effectively.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will see student-led demonstrations of life-skills and peer-mentoring tools capable of deployment on return to their institutions.
- Participants will understand the selection, training and supervision requirements of a credible peer-mentoring programme.
- Institutions will be enabled to establish structured social-emotional partnership among students as a standing element of campus provision.
- Student perspectives on campus wellbeing will be captured for the Conclave record.

#### Session 14: Presentations by Three Identified CFIs
*Student well-being surveys: key findings and statistical insights · Day 2 | 12.45–13.15 hrs*

**Brief:** Three Centrally Funded Institutions (CFIs) identified for the purpose will present the key findings of student wellbeing surveys undertaken on their campuses, together with the associated anonymised statistical insights. The presentations will cover survey design and methodology, the principal patterns observed, the issues the findings bring to light and the measures the institutions have taken or propose in response. The session will demonstrate how systematic and ethically conducted surveys can provide an evidence base for institutional planning and for policy.

**Expected outcomes:**
- Participants will understand the patterns of student wellbeing emerging from institutional survey data.
- Participants will appreciate the requirements of sound survey methodology, including ethical conduct, anonymity and the responsible use of data.
- Institutions will be encouraged to undertake periodic wellbeing surveys and to use the findings in institutional planning.
- Inputs will be generated towards a common approach to the assessment of student wellbeing across HEIs.

#### Session 15: Valedictory Session
*Key insights, recommendations and a roadmap for advancing institutional well-being in HEIs · Day 2 | 13.15–14.00 hrs*

**Brief:** The valedictory session will draw together the principal insights and recommendations emerging from the two days of deliberation: the exhibition, panel discussions, symposium, stakeholder breakaway sessions, focused workshops, student presentations and survey findings. It will set out a roadmap for advancing institutional wellbeing in HEIs, identifying priority actions for institutions and areas for further policy consideration, and will formally close the Conclave.

**Expected outcomes:**
- A consolidated statement of the key insights and recommendations of the Conclave will be presented.
- A roadmap of priority actions for advancing institutional wellbeing in HEIs will be articulated.
- Areas requiring further policy consideration by the Ministry of Education will be identified.
- The proceedings and recommendations of the Conclave will be documented for dissemination to participating institutions and for follow-up.
